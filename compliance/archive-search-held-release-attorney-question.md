# Archive Search — Quick Question: Releasing Legal-Hold Items

**Prepared for:** Peter McKenzie, Rincon Management, to forward to outside counsel
**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Purpose:** one short, specific question — not a re-review of anything already approved.

---

Archive search has a separate "held" category, distinct from the Fair Housing screening you've already reviewed: emails automatically excluded because they look like attorney correspondence or another real legal matter. Right now, once something is held, there is no way to move it back into search — a stronger, permanent exclusion, unlike the Fair Housing flags, which do have a reviewed release path.

Rincon wants to add the same kind of release path for held items: a human reviews a held item and, if it's not actually privileged/legal material (or otherwise appropriate), restores it to search — logged, attributed, reversible-in-record only, same safeguards as the Fair Housing release mechanism.

**The relevant fact:** all of this content comes from Rincon's shared team email inboxes (Missive), not from any personal or named attorney-communication channel. Real legal matters at Rincon are handled through separate, named company inboxes (Peter's or the Director of Operations'), not the shared inbox this tool searches. Everything in the shared inbox — including anything the hold-check catches — is already fully visible today to everyone with access to that shared inbox, a broader group than the small number of trained employees who would ever have archive-search access.

**The question:** does that fact change your view on whether held items can safely have a release mechanism, the same way it supported your conclusion for the Fair Housing side? We want to check specifically because privilege isn't just an internal risk call — how broadly a privileged item has already circulated can matter to whether privilege holds up if challenged later, and that's your call to make, not ours to assume.
