# Archive Search — Expanding Search Access Through Property 360: Outside Counsel's Opinion

**Date received:** 2026-09-24
**Context:** Response to
`compliance/archive-search-property-360-access-expansion-attorney-question.md`,
which asked whether the standing framework from the 9/23 opinion extends
to expanding Archive Search's authorized population from 2 named admins
to everyone with Property 360 access (~9 active Hub logins today,
uncapped, growing with headcount), and if not, whether some middle
ground (a real intermediate tier, baseline training) would work. Pasted
into chat by Peter; saved here verbatim for the record before governance
review. This is the missing attorney-review prong Asimov's and Mason's

**This is the opinion as received from Peter, reproduced verbatim below

> **Question Presented**
>
> Rincon Management currently limits direct Archive Search access to two
> named administrators.
> Rincon proposes adding Archive Search functionality to Property 360,
> its per-property operational summary page.
> Property 360 is currently available to approximately nine employees
> with Hub credentials. The employee population will change over time as
> personnel are hired or leave the company.
> The proposed search box would allow those employees to search
> historical communications associated with the property rather than
> limiting that functionality to the two existing Archive Search
> administrators.
> The question is whether this broader employee access requires a
> special Archive Search admission process, individual vetting, or a new
> intermediate permission tier.
> A related question is whether this changes the standing guidance
> previously provided concerning future Archive Search iterations.
>
> **Preliminary Opinion**
>
> I would permit the proposed Property 360 integration.
> The expansion from two Archive Search administrators to the employees
> who legitimately use Property 360 is a change in access and therefore
> warrants consideration under the framework established in my prior
> opinions.
> Having considered that issue, however, I do not believe Rincon is
> legally required to restrict historical property email search to two
> specially designated administrators.
> Nor have I identified a requirement that every employee receive
> individualized legal or compliance approval before being permitted to
> search company communications reasonably related to properties the
> employee services.
> I would therefore permit Rincon to make the Property 360 search
> function available to ordinary authorized Hub users whose job
> responsibilities reasonably include access to property, owner, tenant,
> maintenance, leasing, accounting, or related operational information.
> I would not require a separate Archive Search admission committee or
> individualized legal approval.
>
> **1. The Increase From Two Users to Nine Is Not Inherently Problematic**
>
> The number of users should not be treated as the controlling legal
> question.
> The more relevant questions are:
> Who are the users?
> Why are they accessing the information?
> Is the access reasonably related to Rincon's business?
> Nine Rincon employees using an internal property-management system is
> materially different from providing access to:
> * owners;
> * tenants;
> * vendors;
> * members of the public;
> * unrelated outside companies; or
> * employees with no legitimate operational reason to access property
>   information.
> Accordingly, I would not impose an arbitrary numerical ceiling on
> Archive Search.
> Two users are not inherently compliant while nine are inherently risky.
> The appropriate inquiry is whether the employee population has a
> legitimate business reason for access.
>
> **2. Property 360 Is a Logical Place for Historical Property
> Information**
>
> The proposed implementation also matters.
> Rincon is not proposing to publish the entire company email archive to
> every employee.
> The proposed feature exists within Property 360, a property-centered
> operational system.
> Historical communications concerning a property may be useful for
> understanding:
> * maintenance history;
> * owner instructions;
> * tenant communications;
> * prior repairs;
> * leasing history;
> * vendor issues;
> * accommodation history;
> * recurring property problems;
> * prior decisions;
> * promises or commitments;
> * unresolved matters; and
> * general institutional knowledge.
> Allowing employees responsible for operating those properties to locate
> that information is a legitimate business purpose.
> I therefore see no reason to treat historical email search as
> inherently limited to administrators merely because email may contain
> sensitive information.
>
> **3. Fair Housing Law Does Not Require Employees to Be Shielded From
> Protected-Class Information**
>
> This is consistent with my prior opinions.
> Property-management employees necessarily encounter information
> concerning protected characteristics.
> Employees may legitimately know that:
> * a tenant has children;
> * a resident uses a housing voucher;
> * a tenant requested an accommodation;
> * a resident has an approved service or support animal;
> * someone requires accessible parking;
> * a resident speaks another language; or
> * another Fair Housing-related circumstance exists.
> Indeed, employees sometimes need this information to comply with
> Rincon's obligations.
> The compliance issue is generally not whether an employee knows the
> information.
> It is whether Rincon improperly uses protected information or treats
> someone differently because of a protected characteristic.
> Accordingly, I would not restrict Property 360 merely because Archive
> Search may surface Fair Housing-related information.
>
> **4. Successful Accommodation Information Can Be Operationally
> Important**
>
> Disability accommodation records illustrate why excessive restriction
> can actually make the system less useful.
> Suppose an old communication establishes:
> "Resident has an approved accommodation requiring 48-hour notice before
> non-emergency maintenance entry."
> The employee scheduling maintenance may have a legitimate operational
> reason to find that information.
> Likewise:
> "Reserved accessible parking space approved."
> or:
> "Assistance animal accommodation approved."
> may be information operational staff should know.
> The objective should therefore not be preventing employees from
> encountering protected information.
> The objective should be ensuring employees use information
> appropriately.
>
> **5. I Would Not Require Individual Vetting of Every Property 360
> User**
>
> I would not require Rincon management or counsel to individually
> approve every employee who receives access.
> That would create an unnecessary administrative process.
> Instead, I would permit access based upon ordinary job role.
> For example, if Rincon determines that an employee's normal
> responsibilities justify Property 360 access, management may also
> permit that employee to use the historical property-search function.
> That determination may be made through Rincon's normal employee
> provisioning process.
> I would not require a separate legal determination.
>
> **6. A Separate Intermediate Archive Search Tier Is Optional, Not
> Required**
>
> Rincon specifically asks whether it needs an intermediate permission
> tier between:
> full Archive Search administrator
> and
> ordinary Property 360 user.
> I do not consider such a tier legally necessary.
> It may be useful technically if administrators possess capabilities
> ordinary users should not have — for example, system configuration,
> bulk export, deletion, classifier modification, or unrestricted
> cross-property administration.
> In that case, ordinary users can simply receive search privileges
> without receiving administrative privileges.
> But that is ordinary role-based access design.
> It does not require a special compliance bureaucracy.
> A simple model would be:
> Administrator
> May configure/manage Archive Search.
> Ordinary authorized employee
> May search information reasonably necessary for ordinary
> property-management responsibilities.
> That is sufficient in my view.
>
> **7. Baseline Training Is Sufficient**
>
> I would not create a special Archive Search certification program
> unless Rincon independently finds it useful.
> Employees who handle tenant and applicant information should already
> understand Rincon's Fair Housing requirements.
> California law applies directly to property managers and
> housing-related services, and current CRD guidance identifies
> discrimination, discriminatory screening, failure to accommodate,
> source-of-income discrimination, harassment and retaliation among the
> relevant prohibited conduct.
> The important training principle for Archive Search is straightforward:
> Information appearing in Archive Search may provide operational
> context, but protected characteristics may not be used as a basis for
> unlawful differential treatment or housing decisions.
> That principle can be incorporated into Rincon's ordinary Fair Housing
> and system-use training.
> I would not require employees to complete a separate course every time
> another internal information source becomes available.
>
> **8. No Special Admission Process Is Required**
>
> I would permit Rincon to use its existing Hub-account provisioning as
> the access mechanism if management is comfortable that Hub accounts
> are issued only to personnel with legitimate business responsibilities.
> In other words:
> employee receives appropriate Hub access -> Property 360 becomes
> available -> property search becomes available.
> I would not require:
> employee hired -> separate Archive Search application -> compliance
> review -> individual approval -> special legal authorization ->
> Archive Search access.
> The second process adds administrative complexity without an obvious
> corresponding legal requirement.
>
> **9. Automatic Headcount Growth Is Also Acceptable**
>
> The fact that the user population may grow as Rincon hires additional
> employees does not materially change my conclusion.
> Rincon does not need to return to counsel when the ninth user becomes
> the tenth, or the tenth becomes the fifteenth.
> The relevant limitation should be role and legitimate business purpose,
> not headcount.
> Accordingly, future employees may receive the same access through
> Rincon's normal provisioning process when their job responsibilities
> justify Property 360 access.
>
> **10. Logging Is Useful but Should Not Become a Permission Gate**
>
> I would continue ordinary system logging.
> Logging provides accountability and allows Rincon to investigate
> unusual use if a problem arises.
> But I would not require management to:
> * approve every search;
> * review search logs routinely;
> * require employees to enter a justification before searching;
> * obtain compliance approval before viewing results; or
> * require human review of results before employees may see them.
> Those controls would materially undermine the usefulness of an
> operational search system.
> Logging plus ordinary management oversight is sufficient absent
> evidence of misuse.
>
> **11. I Would Preserve the Distinction Between Search and Consequential
> Reliance**
>
> This remains the most useful safeguard from the prior opinions.
> Employees may search and retrieve historical information without
> preapproval.
> If historical information is going to become the material basis for a
> significant adverse action — particularly an applicant denial,
> termination of tenancy, accommodation denial, or another consequential
> housing decision — the employee should follow Rincon's normal
> procedures and independently evaluate the relevant facts.
> That does not mean every search result needs human review.
> It means:
> search broadly; exercise professional judgment; apply the appropriate
> substantive process when making consequential decisions.
>
> **12. Privacy Does Not Require Maximum Internal Restriction**
>
> The fact that historical communications may contain personal
> information does not, by itself, mean only two employees may access
> them.
> Privacy controls should be reasonable and proportionate to the
> business purpose.
> Property-management companies necessarily permit employees to access
> tenant, applicant, owner and property information in order to operate.
> The relevant principle should be legitimate internal business use
> rather than artificial minimization of employee headcount.
> Accordingly, I would not interpret general privacy principles as
> requiring Rincon to keep Archive Search confined to two administrators
> when a larger operational group legitimately needs the information.
>
> **13. Standing Guidance for Future Access Questions**
>
> This opinion modifies and clarifies the earlier standing guidance
> concerning changes in user population.
> The prior reference to a "materially broader employee population"
> should not be interpreted to mean that every addition of employees or
> every expansion of internal access requires new legal review.
> That would be unnecessarily restrictive.
> The better standing rule is:
> Rincon may expand internal access to Archive Search and related
> information systems when management reasonably determines that the
> additional employees have a legitimate business reason for access and
> remain subject to Rincon's confidentiality, Fair Housing, privacy, and
> information-use policies.
> This includes:
> * adding additional property managers;
> * adding maintenance personnel whose duties require property history;
> * adding leasing personnel;
> * adding accounting personnel where relevant;
> * adding operations personnel;
> * providing Archive Search functionality through other internal
>   systems;
> * expanding Property 360 functionality;
> * changing role definitions;
> * creating or eliminating intermediate access tiers; and
> * automatically provisioning access based upon an approved job role.
> Those changes should ordinarily be management decisions rather than
> matters requiring a new legal opinion.
>
> **14. When an Access Expansion Should Come Back for Review**
>
> I would reserve additional legal/privacy review for materially
> different situations.
> Examples include:
> * providing Archive Search to owners or tenants;
> * providing access to unrelated outside companies;
> * allowing vendors broad access to tenant communications;
> * making archive information publicly accessible;
> * allowing employees with no legitimate business purpose to browse
>   sensitive information;
> * materially changing the information's purpose;
> * using historical protected-class information to automate
>   consequential housing decisions; or
> * providing unrestricted access to highly sensitive information
>   outside personnel who reasonably need it.
> Those circumstances raise different issues.
> Ordinary expansion among Rincon employees performing Rincon's
> property-management operations generally does not.
>
> **15. Clarification of Prior Standing Opinions**
>
> For avoidance of doubt, my previous opinions should not be interpreted
> as establishing:
> two named administrators
> or
> eight specifically approved employees
> as a permanent legal limitation on Archive Search.
> Those numbers described the system as it existed or was anticipated at
> the time.
> They were not intended to establish a legal ceiling.
> The governing principle is legitimate business use by authorized
> Rincon personnel.
> Accordingly, the standing guidance should now be understood as
> permitting Rincon management to determine which employee roles
> reasonably require access without seeking a new legal opinion every
> time that population changes.
>
> **Specific Opinion**
>
> I approve adding Archive Search functionality to Property 360 for
> appropriate Rincon employees.
> I would not require access to remain limited to the two existing
> administrators.
> I would not require individual counsel approval.
> I would not require a separate compliance admission process.
> I would not require an elaborate intermediate-access tier solely for
> legal reasons.
> If Rincon wishes to distinguish ordinary search users from
> administrators because administrators possess configuration or other
> elevated capabilities, that is sensible role-based system design, but
> it is principally an operational/security decision.
> I would permit ordinary Property 360 users with legitimate
> property-management responsibilities to use the search functionality
> through their existing authenticated Hub accounts.
>
> **Standing Opinion**
>
> Going forward, management may reasonably expand or contract Archive
> Search access among Rincon employees according to legitimate job
> responsibilities without obtaining renewed legal approval for each
> change.
> The standing question should be:
> Does this employee or role have a legitimate Rincon business reason to
> access this information?
> If yes, management may ordinarily authorize access.
> The question should not be:
> Was this particular employee individually approved when Archive Search
> was originally designed?
> Nor should Rincon assume that every additional employee increases
> legal risk enough to require another technical restriction.
> Trained employees and professional judgment remain legitimate
> components of Rincon's compliance framework.
>
> **Bottom Line**
>
> Yes. I would permit the Property 360 expansion.
> Moving from two specifically named Archive Search administrators to a
> larger group of Rincon employees does technically broaden access, but
> I do not believe that fact alone requires Rincon to maintain a special
> access gate.
> The appropriate boundary is legitimate business need, not an arbitrary
> number of users.
> Rincon may therefore make property-level historical search available
> through Property 360 to employees whose ordinary responsibilities
> justify access, using its normal authenticated employee-account
> process.
> Baseline Fair Housing/confidentiality training, ordinary logging,
> management supervision and an escalation path are reasonable
> safeguards.
> A separate legal review should generally be reserved for materially
> different access — particularly external access, access without a
> legitimate business purpose, materially different use of the
> information, or use of sensitive information to automate consequential
> housing decisions.
> This clarification should be treated as part of the standing Archive
> Search framework for future iterations.
