# Complaint Tracking — Quick Question: Identifying a Complaint's Tenant/Owner by Name, With Mandatory Human Confirmation

**Prepared for:** Peter McKenzie, Rincon Management, to forward to outside counsel
**Date:** 2026-10-02
**Purpose:** one specific new question about a not-yet-built proposal — not a re-review of the already-cleared Archive Search / Complaint Tracking pipeline (`compliance/archive-search-significance-complaint-merge-mason-review.md`), and not covered by the standing guidance in `compliance/fair-housing-standing-counsel-guidance.md` (that guidance is scoped to information-retrieval/classification changes; this proposal would, for the first time, use AI-read content to decide *which real person* a record is about).

---

**What's already built, for context:** today, a complaint record is only ever attached to a specific tenant or owner when a participant's email address matches one, unique record on file — deterministic, no AI involved. When no address matches, the record is left unattached rather than guessed. This is a deliberate rule, stated the same way in four separate places in the system (a code comment, a database column description, a second code comment, and the engineering spec): a name alone is never enough to attach a record to a real person, because doing so for a Fair Housing-adjacent record carries real risk if the system gets it wrong. The AI is already allowed to identify a *property* or a *vendor* by name from an email's content — never a tenant or owner.

**What's new:** about 70% of complaints can't be matched to a real tenant or owner today because no address matched (even after a separate fix shipped today that recovers some of that gap). Peter wants to close the rest of that gap by having the AI read the email and name the tenant or owner it believes the thread is about, when it's confident. Under the version being proposed, nothing would be saved automatically — a staff member would have to confirm the AI's suggested match before it's recorded as that person's complaint. We checked Rincon's own tenant and owner lists directly: about 2% of tenants and 2.5% of owners share an exact full name with a different, unrelated person also on file. So a real complaint could end up permanently recorded against the wrong real person — someone who did nothing — even with a human clicking "confirm," if the confirming employee has no more to go on than the same name the AI saw.

**The question:** does adding this second, name-based way of attaching a complaint to a real tenant or owner — even with mandatory human confirmation before anything is saved — create Fair Housing or other legal exposure that the existing address-only rule was specifically built to avoid? We're asking because the measured name-collision rate above is real, not theoretical, and because this system is deliberately designed so that a complaint's attached tenant/owner could matter to some future, not-yet-built tool (a screening, renewal, or similar review) — so getting the wrong real person attached today could follow that person later.
