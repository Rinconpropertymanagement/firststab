# Rincon Management — 2026 Content Strategy

**Purpose:** Marketing content to attract **new owners** — not tenants.
**Status:** Draft for review
**Owner:** Peter McKenzie (write + approve) · Jackie (post + publish)

---

## 1. Goal

No hard number target for 2026. The goal is **consistent visibility and lead generation among prospective owners**, with a specific focus on convincing **self-managing landlords** to hand off management to Rincon.

This lines up with the existing marketing plan's stated objective: *"Convince a self-managing landlord to use a PM company, and to use Rincon."* Self-managing landlords make up an estimated 66% of investors in our market — the single biggest pool of prospects who aren't yet using any property manager, let alone us.

---

## 2. Who We're Talking To

Two owner personas, both already defined in the marketing plan — no tenant-facing content in this calendar.

**Alice — the Accidental Landlord.** A homeowner who ended up renting out a property and is overwhelmed by managing it herself. She wants peace of mind and doesn't want to lose money or control by handing it off. Content for Alice should reduce overwhelm and build trust — plain language, real stories, no jargon.

**Ivan — the Investor.** A self-managing (or switching) investor who wants better returns and market insight, not just a hands-off experience. He responds to numbers, benchmarks, and proof — not just reassurance.

For both, the specific wedge is: *what is self-managing actually costing you, and what would you get back if you didn't have to?*

---

## 3. Core Angle

Pulled from the existing brand and marketing materials — this isn't new positioning, just applied specifically to self-managing landlords:

- **The hidden cost of DIY.** Time, legal risk, and missed income that self-managing landlords don't measure but feel.
- **Expertise as reassurance, not a sales pitch.** Local market knowledge, screening rigor, and legal compliance — framed as "here's what you're up against" rather than "here's why we're great."
- **Transparency, since it's underused.** Past content has leaned heavily on Expertise; Transparency (no hidden fees, clear reporting) shows up far less often and is worth pushing harder here, since it's a real differentiator and a real self-managing-landlord objection ("what am I actually paying for").

Every piece should map to one of the two existing content pillars:
1. **Stress-Free Property Management** — the case for handing it off.
2. **Optimizing Real Estate Performance** — the case for doing better than DIY.

---

## 4. Channels

- **Blog / website** — the primary format historically (educational articles, ~79% of past content). Doubles as SEO and something to link from social.
- **Long-form video** — posted to the existing Accidental Landlord YouTube channel. Expands on the same topic as its paired blog post, in Peter's own voice.
- **Social media** — used to distribute the blog and video, plus shorter standalone takes (a stat, a myth-bust, a quick tip) aimed at self-managing landlords specifically.

Every two weeks, one blog post, one long-form video, and a small batch of social posts go out together, all built around the same topic.

Email/nurture campaigns already exist as a separate ongoing program under the marketing plan's ABM effort and aren't part of this calendar.

---

## 5. Q3 2026 Content Calendar (Biweekly)

Scoped to **this quarter only** (7/16–9/24, 6 cycles) rather than the full year, so we can run it, see what actually generates leads, and plan Q4 off real results instead of guessing months ahead.

Legal/compliance content is deliberately left out — that's already handled on its own cadence by content-engine's legal-update scanning (see Section 7).

Topics are sequenced deliberately, not just rotated evenly, to maximize interest and leads:

1. **Open with the pain point** (why a self-managing landlord should keep reading)
2. **Make it a direct comparison** (the highest-intent search topic there is: "should I self-manage or hire a PM")
3. **Back it with a concrete, scary number** (loss aversion — a real vacancy example)
4. **Solve a tactical problem they're Googling right now** (SEO-driven, high search intent)
5. **Prove it with a real story** (per the marketing plan's own funnel data, testimonials/success stories are the strongest decision-stage, lead-converting content type — stronger than more education)
6. **Close the quarter with a direct offer/CTA** to convert the interest built up over the prior five pieces

## 6. Workflow

1. **Peter** writes the blog draft, hosts/records the video, and gives final approval on everything.
2. **Jackie** edits the video and posts/publishes across blog, video, and social once approved.
3. No piece goes out without Peter's sign-off first.

---

## 7. How Content Gets Made

We already have the tools for the blog and social legs of this — no new build needed there.

1. **content-engine** (`projects/content-engine`) drafts the blog post and social captions, grounded in the legal compliance knowledge base, and writes them to Supabase. It never publishes anything on its own.
2. **content-review** (`projects/content-review`) is where Peter reviews each draft — approve, request changes, or reject. Nothing goes live without going through this step first.
3. Once a blog post is approved, it becomes the **outline/script basis for that cycle's video** — Peter records it in his own voice rather than reading it verbatim.
4. **Jackie** edits the video, then posts the blog, video, and approved social captions.

Video itself isn't something the content engine can generate — it has to be Peter on camera — so this is the one step in the pipeline that stays fully manual.
