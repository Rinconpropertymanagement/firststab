## What I noticed, objectively

**Sourcing:** content-engine's draft links to two of Rincon's own existing blog posts (good internal linking/SEO) but states its key numbers (8-12% fee, 14-20% all-in, the vacancy-cost math) without hyperlinking any of them to an outside source. My manual draft hyperlinks every specific stat to the real external source it came from (Shuk, iPropertyManagement, Baselane, RentalCalcs).

**Format compliance:** content-engine's draft is missing the standard "About Rincon" closing boilerplate that all 185 of your past posts have used — worth fixing before it goes out, since consistency there is part of the house style. It also has a redundant "Related reading" mention with two dead `#` links right above the real, working Related Reading section at the bottom — cosmetic issue, easy fix.

**Flagged uncertainty:** content-engine's draft includes an inline `[NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW: California/Ventura County eviction notice periods...]` flag on the eviction-cost claim — it's being appropriately cautious about a legal-adjacent detail rather than guessing. That flag needs your input (either confirm/soften the language, or leave the eviction-cost figure as a general national stat without implying it's CA-specific).

**Voice:** both lean into the direct, "frankly"/"honest math" register from your brand guide. content-engine's version reads slightly more conversational/first-person; my manual draft is a bit closer to the third-person house style with the standard byline and closing.

## What this doesn't tell you

This is one topic, one comparison. content-engine's real advantage is that it's repeatable at scale without me hand-researching and hand-writing every piece — that's the whole point of having it. If you like the manual draft's sourcing and format better, the more useful fix is probably improving content-engine's prompt/rules (require the About Rincon boilerplate, require external citations for stated stats) rather than hand-writing every future post.
